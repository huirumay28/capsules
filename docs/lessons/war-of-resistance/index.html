1937–1945: when the world war started in China

Marco Polo Bridge to surrender — a war already eight years old when many textbooks open in Poland.

16 August 2026 1937–1945 (with 1931 behind it) About 16 minutes

This capsule is the Second World War from China, not a European war with an Asian chapter. Death-toll figures for Nanjing remain contested; the text says so instead of picking a round number for effect. One photograph of a bombed street is enough. There are no pictures of bodies used as decoration.

Before

A railway charge, a puppet state, a capital that still believed in time

On the night of 1931 a section of the South Manchuria Railway near Mukden was blown up. The blamed Chinese saboteurs and occupied the Northeast. Later evidence treats the blast as a Japanese pretext. The League of Nations sent the . The 1932 report said Japan had not acted in self-defense and that was not a real independence movement. The League adopted the report. It did not put the army back. Japan left the League in 1933.

In those same years the Nationalist government was trying to be a state from Nanjing — the “,” 1927–1937: ministries, a currency, campaigns against the Communists, a capital that still looked like a future. The decade is a historian’s interval. It ends when the capital has to move.

The era

A night at a bridge, then a country on the road

On 7 July 1937 Japanese troops on night exercise near the (), southwest of Beijing, reported a missing soldier and shots at Wanping. The soldier turned up. The firing did not become a local incident. Within weeks it was a war. Shanghai was fought over from August to November. The government left Nanjing.

Japanese forces entered Nanjing on 13 December 1937. For weeks into January 1938 there were mass killings, rape, and looting. The International Military Tribunal for the Far East later cited a death toll of over 200,000; the official figure of the People’s Republic is 300,000. Historians still argue about the arithmetic. They do not argue about whether the weeks happened. This capsule uses one photograph of a street already bombed in September — not a picture of corpses.

The capital that remained was : fog, stairs, and, from 1938 into 1943, repeated Japanese bombing. It was one of the long air campaigns of the war against a civilian city. People dug shelters into the rock. The government that sat there was still Chiang’s.

The put the Nationalists and the Communists in the same war against Japan. It was a cease-fire with a shared enemy, not a merger. Zhou Enlai spent much of the war as the Communist representative in the Nationalist capitals. The front frayed. It did not wait for 1945 to fail.

Across occupied Asia the Japanese military ran a system of sexual slavery later named / : stations, contracts that were not contracts, women from Korea, China, the Philippines, and elsewhere. It was a regional system, not a single atrocity attached to one city. The sources are documents and survivors. This capsule does not put a face on it for layout.

Look outward from China, not the other way around. On 1 September 1939 Germany invaded Poland. That is a real beginning — of the European war. It is not the beginning of this one. For two years the China war and the European war run in parallel. They join after 7 December 1941, when Japan attacks Pearl Harbor and the Western colonies in Southeast Asia, and the United States declares war. 1941 is the year the wars become one Allied war. It is not the year China entered.

People

A generalissimo, a liaison, a writer, and the unnamed

Chiang Kai-shek 蔣介石

1887–1975

Head of the Nationalist state and of the army that had to trade space for time. This capsule does not need him to be a hero or a villain. It needs him as the man whose government moved west and whose army did not defeat Japan alone.

Chiang in Shanghai, 1937 — a front-line photograph meant to raise morale. It is a posed closeness to the army, not a biography.Wikimedia Commons / public domain

Zhou Enlai 周恩來

1898–1976

Communist representative in the Nationalist wartime capitals. The United Front’s human hinge. After 1949 he is someone else. Here he is the man in the shared city.

Wuhan, summer 1938: Zhou Enlai and Deng Yingchao with the American journalist Edgar Snow. A United Front photograph — Communist liaison, Nationalist wartime capital, foreign press.Wikimedia Commons / public domain

Xiao Hong 蕭紅

1911–1942

Writer, from the Northeast. The Field of Life and Death; Tales of Hulan River. She fled Harbin, wrote in the same years the armies moved, and died in Hong Kong in 1942. Not a general. A person the war displaced and then outlived.

Xiao Hong. The file is a small period portrait; it is what a free archive has. She died in Japanese-occupied Hong Kong in 1942.Wikimedia Commons / public domain

After

Surrender, then another war, then the argument about memory

Japan surrendered in August 1945. In China the was already a fiction. Full-scale civil war between Nationalists and Communists ran from 1946 to 1949. The People’s Republic was proclaimed in Beijing; the Nationalist government moved to Taiwan. The war against Japan became a founding story for more than one state.

is the after that does not end. The PRC, Taiwan, and Japan do not teach 1937–45 as the same syllabus: different dates of emphasis, different silences, different uses of Nanjing and of . Disagreement about the telling is not the same as disagreement about whether the war occurred. Europe’s 1939 remains the other theater — a real war, started later, that textbooks still use as the world’s first page.

If your textbook begins the world war in 1939, whose war has it decided not to open with?

Fun fact

The date the textbook still uses

A great many English-language textbooks still open the Second World War on 1 September 1939, when Germany invaded Poland. China had been in full-scale war since 7 July 1937, and under occupation in the Northeast since 1931. The 1939 date is not a lie about Europe. It is a habit of where the narrator is standing.
