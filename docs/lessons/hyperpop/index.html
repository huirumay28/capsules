Before

Before hyperpop: robot voices, Eurodance and the internet

did not invent the idea of a machine-made pop voice. In 1997 the American company Antares released , software meant to quietly fix singers who were slightly off key. A year later 's turned the correction all the way up so that her voice jumped between notes like a robot, and the sound became known as the . Through the 2000s artists such as Daft Punk, T-Pain and Kanye West made a normal part of pop and hip-hop.

In Japan, the company Crypton Future Media released in August 2007. is a voicebank, a program that sings any melody you type into it, sold with a cartoon character of a sixteen-year-old girl with turquoise twintails. She later performed concerts as a projected animation. In 2011 , a model from the Harajuku fashion scene, released , written and produced by , who also produced the group . Writers also point to Western precursors. Fans of , a style of YouTube remix that speeds up songs and raises their pitch, were already using the word 'hyperpop' in the late 2000s. The earliest known use of the word is older still: in October 1988 the writer Don Shewey used it in an article about the Scottish band .

Origins

PC Music: a record label as an art project

, born in 1990, studied music at , where he made music with his friend . In June 2013 he founded , which at first existed mainly as a page. Within about a year it had posted 40 songs, some with more than 100,000 plays, without releasing a single physical record. Its first paid download was 's in November 2014.

The label's sound took the shiniest parts of 1990s and 2000s pop and pushed them further. The Guardian heard 'the huge synth blasts favoured by chart-botherers such as Cascada, grime's sub-bass, and 's high-pitched vocal range'. has named Korean and Japanese pop, Japanese culture, and the producers Max Martin and Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis as influences. Vocals were pitched up and chopped until they sounded like cartoons. The label often recorded people who were not trained singers and, in 's words, treated them 'as if they're a major label artist'.

behaved less like a band and more like a long-running piece of conceptual art. Many acts were aliases, so it was hard to tell how many people were involved. Artists such as , (short for ), and had invented built from internet slang and cartoon imagery. Songs borrowed the language of advertising, makeup hauls and corporate branding, so critics could never be sure whether the label was celebrating consumer culture or mocking it. Vice described the acts as 'living installations who put out music'. Critics later called this early sound , and Pitchfork counts it as 's first era.

Reaction in 2014 was sharply divided. Spin named its trend of the year and Fact called it the best label of 2014, while other critics found the high-speed sound artless or irritating. In October 2015 the label announced a partnership with Columbia Records, and in December it released Only You, a collaboration between and the Chinese pop star ().

Deep dive

Sophie: sounds like latex, metal and bubbles

was born in 1985 in Northampton, England. Her father took her to raves when she was very young and played rave cassettes in the car, and her favourite acts were the Pet Shop Boys and the Prodigy. Her single (2013) was the breakthrough: it topped XLR8R's year-end list and placed 17th on Pitchfork's. and Hard followed in 2014, and the singles were collected on in 2015.

built her sounds almost entirely from scratch. Working mainly with an synthesiser and the software , she avoided samples and shaped basic waveforms into sounds she described as resembling 'latex, balloons, bubbles, metal, plastic, [and] elastic'. The Guardian wrote that her work sounded as if 'Aphex Twin had swallowed a 12-year-old girl's account', yet still 'spoke of heartache and yearning'. The New York Times called it 'giddy fun' that also invited listeners 'to consider pop's pleasures, structures and gender expectations'.

For years hid her identity. She masked her voice in interviews, obscured herself in photos, and at one Boiler Room show the drag performer Ben Woozy mimed a DJ set as while she posed as a bodyguard. In October 2017 she released , the first time her own voice and image appeared in her work, and she confirmed that she was transgender. Her album followed in June 2018. The title may be a mishearing of 'I love every person's insides'. It was nominated for the Grammy for Best Dance/Electronic Album, which made one of the first openly transgender artists nominated in that category.

also produced for , Vince Staples, , Madonna, the Japanese singer and the K-pop group , and worked with in 2014. On 30 January 2021, aged 34, she died after falling from a rooftop in Athens, Greece, where she had climbed to take a photograph of the full moon. Her second album, almost finished when she died, was completed by her brother and released in 2024. On her birthday that year, 17 September, Google ran a Doodle in her honour.

Second wave

After a well-received DJ set at the 2019 Minecraft festival Fire Festival, they released their debut album on 31 May 2019, with as its single. The Independent said they took 'to its most extreme, and extremely catchy, conclusions: stadium-sized trap beats processed and distorted to near-destruction, overwrought emo vocals and cascades of ravey arpeggios'. This is an unusual origin for a music genre. Earlier scenes in these lessons, such as Chicago house or riot grrrl, were named by the people in a club, a zine or a newspaper. Analysis

What hyperpop was really about

Most earlier music movements in these lessons fought against something fake. Punk rejected polished stadium rock, and folk revivalists looked for authentic songs. did the opposite. Its took the most artificial parts of pop, such as , ringtone melodies, advertising slogans and plastic textures, and made them louder until they became the point. The Wall Street Journal's Mark Richardson described it as turning pop's artificial parts up to an extreme, a 'cartoonish wall of noise'.

Under the brightness, the lyrics were often sad, anxious or ironic, which is why many listeners heard as music about life lived online. NPR's Sheldon Pearce described it as music 'seeking the sound of the dissociated online self'. When so much of daily life happens through screens, filters and avatars, a voice that is obviously processed may feel more honest than one that pretends to be natural.

  • What changed: it made artificiality and excess into a serious artistic position rather than a guilty pleasure.
  • It gave trans and non-binary artists a central place in a pop movement, and it used vocal processing to question how a 'male' or 'female' voice should sound.
  • It showed that a scene could form almost entirely online, through , , Minecraft and , without a local club or city at its centre.
  • Its limits: the name was partly applied by , the label quickly became a catch-all for any loud internet pop, and many of its most inventive young artists rejected it or did not want fame.

After

Brat summer and what followed

By 2021 the label had stretched so wide that it was losing meaning. In August tweeted 'rip ? discuss'. said the genre was 'officially dead', and said he and were 'working on killing' it, before saying three months later that it 'will never die'. In June 2023, on its tenth anniversary, announced that it would stop releasing new music. Its final release was 's album in December 2023.

Then 's ideas reached the biggest audience they ever had. 's , released on 7 June 2024 and produced with , and others, drew on 2000s English rave music. Its cover was a plain square of what called 'an offensive, off-trend shade of green' with the word in lowercase Arial. A fan-made generator let anyone put their own words on it, and 'Brat summer' became a meme. After tweeted 'kamala IS ', 's campaign account restyled its banner in the same green, though later said the tweet was not meant as a political endorsement. became Metacritic's highest-rated album of 2024, made 'brat' its word of the year, and the album won three Grammy Awards, including Best Dance/Electronic Album, the category had been nominated in six years earlier.
